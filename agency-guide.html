📄 A Guide to Agency Relationships

Understanding agency relationships is a crucial part of any real estate transaction. Kentucky law requires that we discuss this with you. This guide explains the different types of agency and how they impact you.

What is an Agency Relationship?

In real estate, an agency relationship is created when a buyer or seller (the principal) hires a real estate agent (the agent) to represent them in a transaction. The agent is legally obligated to act in the best interest of their principal, providing loyalty, confidentiality, and full disclosure.

Types of Agency in Kentucky

Seller's Agent (Listing Agent): Represents the seller. Their duties include marketing the property, negotiating offers, and advising the seller throughout the process. They owe fiduciary duties to the seller.

Buyer's Agent: Represents the buyer. They help buyers find properties, negotiate purchase agreements, and guide them through inspections and closing. They owe fiduciary duties to the buyer.

Dual Agent: Represents both the buyer and the seller in the same transaction. This can only happen with the written consent of both parties. Dual agents must remain neutral and cannot disclose confidential information from one party to the other.

Designated Agent: In a dual agency situation, the brokerage may assign different agents within the same firm to represent the buyer and seller separately. Each designated agent owes full fiduciary duties to their respective client.

Why This Matters to You

Knowing who is representing whom helps ensure that your rights are protected and that you receive the guidance you need. Whether you are buying or selling, it's important to understand the roles and responsibilities of everyone involved.
